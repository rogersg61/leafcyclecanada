Nature's Second Chance

Turning Fallen Leaves Into Everyday Goods.

We upcycle community leaf waste into durable, beautiful bags and eco-friendly paper products. Support sustainability and empower local students.

The numbers in these worked examples are illustrative model values, not audited LeafCycle measurements. In each equation, constants are assumed starting amounts that do not change with the variable; each dropdown explains what its numbers and operations mean.

Leaves collected4.5L + 12,500 > 2.5L + 2,012,500
  • Scenario: Compare two running estimates of leaves collected.
  • Variable: L is the number of leaves collected.
  • Variable terms: 4.5L means 4.5 times L; 2.5L means 2.5 times L.
  • Constants: +12,500 adds the assumed leaves already in the first tally; +2,012,500 adds the comparison tally's assumed starting count. These amounts do not change with L.
  • Sign: > means the first estimate must be strictly greater than the second.
  • Solving:
    • Subtract 2.5L from both sides: 2L + 12,500 > 2,012,500.
    • Subtract 12,500 from both sides: 2L > 2,000,000.
    • Divide both sides by 2: L > 1,000,000.
  • Result: The estimate requires more than one million leaves.
Carbon footprint3.5C + 125 < 1.5C + 825
  • Scenario: Compare two accounting estimates of the manufacturing footprint.
  • Variable: C is the carbon footprint in kilograms.
  • Variable terms: 3.5C means 3.5 times C; 1.5C means 1.5 times C.
  • Constants: +125 kg adds the first process's assumed setup emissions; +825 kg adds the comparison process's assumed starting emissions. Neither amount changes with C.
  • Sign: < means the first estimate must be strictly less than the second.
  • Solving:
    • Subtract 1.5C from both sides: 2C + 125 < 825.
    • Subtract 125 from both sides: 2C < 700.
    • Divide both sides by 2: C < 350.
  • Result: The modeled footprint is below 350 kg.

How We Make It Happen

From the neighborhood lawn to your hands, our process is 100% sustainable, chemical-free, and community-driven.

1. Collection

Local high school volunteers rake and collect fallen leaves from community yards, preventing them from producing methane in landfills.

Methane prevented4.5M + 12 > 1.5M + 162
  • Scenario: Compare two estimates of methane prevented during a neighborhood sweep.
  • Variable: M is kilograms of methane prevented.
  • Variable terms: 4.5M means 4.5 times M; 1.5M means 1.5 times M.
  • Constants: +12 kg is the first estimate's assumed starting credit; +162 kg is the comparison estimate's assumed starting credit. Neither changes with M.
  • Sign: > means the first estimate must be strictly greater than the second.
  • Solving:
    • Subtract 1.5M from both sides: 3M + 12 > 162.
    • Subtract 12 from both sides: 3M > 150.
    • Divide both sides by 3: M > 50.
  • Result: The model requires more than 50 kg of methane prevented per sweep.

2. Fiber Extraction

Using our eco-friendly mechanical process, we break down the dry leaves into strong, flexible fibers and natural bio-composites.

Harmful chemicals avoided4A + 10 ≥ 2A + 130
  • Scenario: Compare harmful additives avoided with a conventional-process benchmark.
  • Variable: A is grams of harmful additives avoided per batch.
  • Variable terms: 4A means 4 times A; 2A means 2 times A.
  • Constants: +10 g is the first model's assumed starting credit; +130 g is the comparison benchmark's starting amount. Neither changes with A.
  • Sign: ≥ means the first total must be greater than or equal to the benchmark.
  • Solving:
    • Subtract 2A from both sides: 2A + 10 ≥ 130.
    • Subtract 10 from both sides: 2A ≥ 120.
    • Divide both sides by 2: A ≥ 60.
  • Result: The model requires avoiding at least 60 g per batch.

3. Manufacturing

The fibers are pressed and woven into highly durable, aesthetic paper products and reusable shopping bags.

Energy used5E - 30 ≤ 2E + 90

  • Variable: E is energy used, measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh).
  • Variable terms: 5E means 5 times E; 2E means 2 times E.

Building a revolutionary sustainable company isn't easy. Here is the math behind some of the major hurdles we cleared in our first year of operations.

Unpredictable rains heavily affected our collection schedule in the fall. We adapted by buying waterproof gear.

  • Variable: R is the number of rainy days.
  • Variable terms: 5R means 5 times R; 3R means 3 times R.
  • Constants: +8 adds an assumed 8-day allowance to the first estimate; +28 adds a 28-day buffer to the comparison. Neither changes with R.
